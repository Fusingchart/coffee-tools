How to use this coffee ratio calculator

A brew ratio is the weight of ground coffee compared with the weight of water. A 1:16 ratio means 1 gram of coffee for every 16 grams of water. Because one millilitre of water weighs about one gram, you can measure the water in millilitres. Weighing coffee on a kitchen scale is more consistent than using scoops, since beans differ in size and density.

Pick your brew method and the calculator fills in a typical starting ratio. You can then enter the number of cups (we count one cup as 8 US fl oz, about 237 ml), the amount of water you want to use, or the amount of coffee you have. Change the ratio box if you prefer stronger or lighter coffee. A smaller second number, such as 1:15, gives a stronger cup, and a larger one, such as 1:17, gives a lighter cup.

Typical ratios by method

These are common starting points, not rules. Pour-over is often brewed around 1:15 to 1:17, and the Specialty Coffee Association's long-standing guideline for filter coffee is about 1:18, so anywhere in that area is reasonable. French press is commonly made at roughly 1:12 to 1:15. AeroPress recipes vary widely, from about 1:6 for a concentrate you dilute to around 1:16. Espresso uses a much smaller amount of liquid, often 1:2 measured as the liquid that ends up in the cup compared with the dry coffee dose. Cold brew is usually made as a concentrate at about 1:4 to 1:8 and then diluted with water or milk.

Adjusting to taste

If your coffee tastes sour or thin, try a finer grind or a slightly longer brew. If it tastes bitter or harsh, try a coarser grind or a shorter brew. Change one thing at a time and write down what you did, so you can repeat the cups you like.
